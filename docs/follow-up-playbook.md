# Sterling follow-up playbook

Grant Cardone's follow-up system ("The Greatest Sales Secret" and "10
Biggest Follow-Up Mistakes"), rebuilt for a fence staining company that
sells by text, phone and a web proposal and never visits in person.
Brainstormed with Alan on 2026-10-12. Nothing here is live yet; the last
section says what has to be built to run it.

## The rules, in our words

1. **Speed.** A lead gets a human within 5 minutes, not a text within 5
   minutes. The text is already automatic. The call is the thing. Every
   lead gets a price the same day (the attack order).
2. **Frequency.** 80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th touch. Our
   sequences give up after 2 (intake: 24 hours and cold) or 7 (estimate
   sent: 14 days and cold). The new floor is 12 touches in 30 days, then
   one a month for a year.
3. **Variety.** Today every touch is a text from Amy. The new mix: call,
   voicemail, text, photo, video, email, mail, a picture of their own
   fence. Never the same message twice. Never the same channel twice in a
   row.
4. **A reason, every time.** The first line of every touch says why we're
   reaching out. "Just checking in" is banned.
5. **Always leave a message.** No answer is still a touch: voicemail, then
   a text that says we called.
6. **Always agree.** "You're right, it's not cheap. Here's why people do it
   anyway." Never argue price, timing or the HOA.
7. **Present numbers to everyone.** No lead leaves day one without a
   proposal, including the ones who didn't answer the sides question.
8. **Ask for referrals.** "Who do you know with a fence that's going
   gray?" On the proposal, on the after-payment page, on the call. Never
   "if you know anybody, send them my way."
9. **Collect the data.** Every touch logged on the lead. Every reason
   they gave logged as an objection. Every "later" gets a date.
10. **Honest urgency only.** A deadline we hold (the 30-day price, the
    7-day deposit link) beats a fake one. A gift we send beats a gift we
    pretend to send. The customer who catches one lie stops believing
    the rest.

## The mistakes we make today

Measured against the two PDFs and our own data:

- **Not making the call (Cardone's #1).** The "just opened their
  proposal" text fires and nobody dials. 48% of salespeople don't make
  the call; on that alert we're worse than that.
- **Giving up at touch 2.** Intake texts twice in 15 seconds, waits 24
  hours, moves the lead to Cold. That is quitting before touch 3.
- **A hole from day 14 to day 42.** The estimate sequence ends on day 14,
  the nurture starts 28 days later. Four weeks of silence right when
  they're talking to the other two quotes.
- **One channel, one voice.** Seven texts from Amy, no call days, no
  voicemail, no video, no mail, no email, no picture of their fence.
- **"Just checking in."** Three of the current texts open with it.
- **A fake limited-time offer** in the nurture ("20% off for a limited
  time"). Being replaced by the 30-day quoted price that actually ends.
- **No referral ask** anywhere in the funnel until the after-payment
  page, which only the buyers see.
- **No exit survey.** We never ask the lost ones what we missed.
- **No second sale.** House wash, driveway, deck: the chips exist on the
  after-payment page, nothing follows them.

## The sequence

Three clocks. The first starts when the lead arrives. The second starts
when the proposal is sent and runs 30 days, the life of the quoted
price. The third is the year after. A reply stops the clock and hands
the lead to a human for 48 hours, then the clock resumes from where it
was. STOP stops everything. Nothing sends before 8 AM or after 8 PM.

Voices: **Amy** is Olga, the office. **Alan** is the owner. The owner's
touches are the ones that feel personal: video, the management call, the
apology.

### Clock 1: the first hour (lead arrives)

| When | Touch | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Text + photo | Amy (auto) | The intro text and the nearby fence photo. Exists. |
| 0:05 | **Call** | Alan or Amy | Dial within 5 minutes. The dashboard shows a timer on every new lead and turns red at 5:00. No answer: voicemail ("Alan with Sterling, saw your request, I'm pulling up your fence on the map right now, call me back") and a text that says we called. |
| 0:30 | Scope drawing | Amy | The scope-of-work image texted as the discovery call is dialed. Exists. |
| Same day | **Proposal** | Amy | Priced the same day, every lead, sides answered or not. Exists as the attack order. |
| On open | **Call within 90 seconds** | Whoever owns the alert that hour | The "just opened their proposal" text already fires. It gets an owner: a name on the dashboard for each block of the day. The call: "Saw you're looking at it. Which one are you leaning toward?" |

| 100 | Small gift | Email or text | Alan | Only if it's real: a $5 coffee e-gift to the leads who actually talked to us. "Coffee's on me. No strings." Or skip the day. Never a gift we don't send. |

After day 365: one touch a quarter, each one a reason, until they buy,
say stop, or move.

### After the sale (the second sale)

The after-payment page already asks for the score, the review, the
referral and the other services. The follow-up on those:

- Score 9 or 10: the referral ask by text a week later. "Who do you know
  with a fence going gray? $100 for you and for them." Then again at
  the 6-month mark.
- Any service chip tapped: a priced link within 24 hours, by a human.
- Every customer: the rebook text at the package's life. Essential at 12
  months, Signature at 24, Legacy at 36. "It's been two years. The
  Signature on your fence has about a year left. Want the same crew?"

## Scripts for the calls

**No answer, any day.** "Hey, it's Alan with Sterling Fence Staining.
[the reason]. My cell is 346-589-7877, call or text me back." Then the
text: "Just tried you. [the reason in one line]."

**Objection, any call.** Agree first, then the reason people do it
anyway. Price: "You're right, it's not cheap. Replacing it is five
times that, and staining buys you years." Timing: "Makes sense. Which
month? I'll hold the price for it." HOA: "Smart to check. Send me their
color list and I'll match it, most of our colors are already approved."
Spouse: "Of course. Want me to send the proposal to them too so you're
looking at the same thing?"

**The referral ask, word for word.** "Who do you know that would want
their fence looking like yours?" Never "if you know anybody."

## What has to be built to run this

1. **Call tasks with a clock.** New leads show a timer on the Dashboard;
   the call days in the sequence create a task for the named person
   with the script on it; a task not done by end of day lands in the 3
   PM digest. The engine today only sends texts and moves stages.
2. **A name on the proposal-opened alert**, by hour block, so the
   90-second call has an owner.
3. **The fence render.** Their photo, or the scope drawing, with the
   package's color applied. A new piece: the Fence AI we have measures
   fences, it doesn't paint them. This is the single most valuable
   touch in the sequence and worth building first.
4. **Video and voice steps in the engine.** An MMS step that sends a
   stored video with the customer's name in the text, and Alan's weekly
   recording slot.
5. **Nearby-job texts.** The nearby-jobs map exists; the step that
   writes "0.4 miles from you Thursday" from it does not.
6. **Real dated offers in Settings**, so day 50 and the ad say the same
   thing and both end on the date.
7. **Rebuild the two sequences** in the engine: the 30-day close window
   replaces the 14-day estimate sequence; the year replaces the current
   nurture. Delete the "20% off for a limited time" text.
8. **The exit survey and the 1-to-10** saved as objections on the lead,
   so the lost-reason report has data.
9. **Touch count on the lead and on the Dashboard**: how many touches
   this lead has had, which channel, which day of the clock. Close rate
   by touch number is the metric that proves the whole thing.

## What to measure

- Calls made within 5 minutes of the lead, as a percentage. Target 80%.
- Touches before close. Expect most closes between touch 5 and 8 once
  the sequence runs; today most closes are touch 1 to 3 because nothing
  else happens.
- Reply rate by touch and by channel. The fence render and the video
  should beat every text.
- Reopens after day 30 and what happens on the call.
- The year: wins from the nurture per month, and the "why not us"
  answers.
