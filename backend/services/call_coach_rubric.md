# A&T Fence Staining — VA Call Coach Reference

This document is the source of truth for evaluating Olga's lead intake calls.
When Olga uploads a recording, the AI call coach uses this document to compare
what she actually said against what the call is supposed to do. The coach
should give feedback grounded in the purpose and the script below — what went
well, what got missed, and what could have been sharper.

## What the Call Is For

This is **not a sales call**. It's a quick, warm, low-pressure intake call
that prepares us to send an accurate written estimate. Olga is not closing,
not pitching, and not negotiating. The customer will compare prices, decide
on a package, and make their final decision after they've received the
written estimate — not on the phone.

Olga's only job is to:

1. Confirm the customer's profile is correct — name, address, ZIP, fence
   height, fence age, and whether it's been previously stained.
2. **Confirm which sides of the fence they want stained.** This is the part
   of the call that most directly affects estimate accuracy. Misunderstanding
   sides leads to wrong prices, wrong measurements, and bad customer
   experiences when the crew arrives.
3. Optionally explain the three packages — but only if the customer wants
   the explanation. Most customers prefer to review the packages on the
   written estimate. Olga should never force a package walkthrough.
4. Set clear expectations that the estimate will be measured on Google Earth
   and texted to them within a few minutes.
5. End the call warmly with no pressure to commit, decide, or schedule.

If Olga executes those five things in 3–5 minutes, the call was successful —
even if the customer didn't say much, didn't pick a package, and didn't ask
any questions.

## What "Success" Looks Like on a Call

The AI coach should evaluate Olga against these criteria:

- The customer profile is verified (or, if missing, gathered) — especially
  the address and ZIP.
- The fence sides are confirmed clearly and read back to the customer.
- The package walkthrough is offered, not forced — and skipped gracefully
  if the customer says "just send it."
- The cleaning explanation is given correctly based on fence age (skip
  cleaning fee for fences 0–6 months; explain the chemical wash for fences
  6+ months).
- **No pricing is quoted on the call.** Pricing only ever appears on the
  written estimate.
- **No specific job dates are committed.** Scheduling is handled by the
  project manager after the customer accepts the estimate.
- Anything Olga doesn't know is escalated with the line: "That's a great
  question for our project manager. I'll have him reach out to you after
  this call."
- The call stays warm, brief, and natural — Olga shouldn't sound like she's
  reading a script, even though she's following one.

If any of those are missed, the coach should call it out — kindly,
specifically, and with what to do differently next time.

## What Counts as "Off-Script" vs. "Going Off-Script Well"

Following the script word-for-word is not the goal. The goal is to hit every
stage of the script in order, in Olga's natural voice, while keeping the
call short and warm. Some natural variation is good — robotic recitation
is bad.

- ✅ **Acceptable:** Olga rephrases the greeting in a warmer way, asks the
  address differently, or skips the package walkthrough because the customer
  said "just send the estimate."
- ❌ **Not acceptable:** Olga skips confirming sides, quotes a price, commits
  to a job date, forces a package walkthrough on a customer who said no, or
  guesses at an answer she doesn't know instead of escalating.

The coach should reward good judgment when Olga adapts the script to the
customer, and push back when she drifts off-mission (sales pitching,
over-explaining, wandering off the workflow).

## The Script

### Stage 1 — Greeting

> Hi, is this (___)? Hi [___]! This is [YOUR NAME] calling from A&T's
> Fence Staining — we just received your information. How are you doing?

Customer: I'm doing great, how are you?
Me: I'm doing great
Customer: I'm sorry who is this?
Me: This is Amy with A&T's Fence Staining

### Stage 2 — Address Confirmation

> I just want to call to make sure everything we have on file is correct
> so that I can send your estimate.

**IF they put an address:** I have your address as [ADDRESS, ZIP]. Is that
correct? (update it if anything is incorrect)

**If NO address on file:** What's the address where you'd like the fence
staining done? And what's the ZIP code there? I want to make sure we get
an accurate measurement using google earth. Just to confirm — that's
[FULL ADDRESS, ZIP]

(if they don't want to give you an address then ask them for the linear
feet so we can give them a rough estimate)

### Stage 3 — Fence Details

**Fence Height:**
- If filled in: I just wanted to confirm that your fence is (___)ft tall?
  ex: (six, six and a half, seven, seven and a half)
- If not filled in: (confirm it for yourself later and don't talk to them
  about it)

**Fence Age:**
- If filled in: I see here that your fence is between (___) years old?
- If not filled in: About how old would you say the fence is?

**Previously Stained:**
- If filled in: And (it is not previously / is previously) stained right?
- If not filled in: (confirm it for yourself later and don't talk to them
  about it)

**Fence Sides:**
> Okay and one last thing! — which sides of the fence do you want stained?
> Which sides?

8 sides that they could possibly want stained:
- Inside front (which usually contains a gate)
- Inside left
- Inside back
- Inside right
- Outside front (usually contains a gate)
- Outside left
- Outside back
- Outside right

Once they tell you, **confirm it back to them**:
> Okay so to confirm you want the (name the sides)?

### Stage 4 — Package Walkthrough (Cleaning Lead-In)

**If fence is brand new (0–6 months):**
> Ok perfect, since your fence is brand new, it will not require a cleaning
> before we stain it. I will make sure to take that charge off the cleaning
> fee since it won't be needed.

**If fence is older than 6 months:**
> Ok since your fence is over 6 months old, it will need to be cleaned before
> we stain it. We use a biodegradable chemical wash that is safe for the
> plants and pets. It removes the grey color, sprinkler stains, and any mold
> and we also pressure wash if it's needed. This will make sure the stain
> penetrates the wood properly.

### Stage 5 — Explain the Packages (Only If Customer Wants)

> Once we clean and stain it we will apply the stain based on your chosen
> package which we will have on the estimate. **Would you like me to explain
> the packages or would you prefer to review them when I send the estimate?**

If they want the explanation:

- **Essential** — clear stain that protects the fence and lasts one to three
  years.
- **Signature Finish** — most popular package, rich colors with more of a
  natural look that lasts three to six years.
- **Legacy Premium** — solid and bold color, maximum protection, typically
  lasts three to eight years.

> Okay and just to let you know every package comes with 2 coats of stain
> and we also back brush the stain as well.

### Stage 6 — Set Estimate Expectations

> Perfect, once I get off the call I'm going to measure your fence on Google
> Earth and then I'll text you your estimate here in a few minutes. Do you
> have any questions before I send you the estimate?

### Stage 7 — Goodbye

> Okay thank you, have a great day!

**If you don't know the answer:**
> That's a great question for our project manager. I'll have him reach out
> to you after this call!

## How the Coach Should Use This Document

When reviewing a call recording, the coach should:

1. Walk through the call in order and check whether each stage was hit.
2. Quote specific moments from the recording when giving feedback — not
   vague summaries.
3. Reward good judgment, not robotic recitation.
4. Flag any boundary violations (price quoting, scheduling commitments,
   guessing at answers) immediately and clearly.
5. **End every review with one specific, actionable thing Olga can do
   better next call. Just one. Not a list.**

The goal of every call review is the same as the goal of every call: keep
it short, useful, and focused on what actually moves the business forward.
